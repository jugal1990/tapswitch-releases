Setting it up, on the phone

No computer, no cable, no scripts. Android hides the switch TapSwitch needs behind Developer options, so you turn that on once and the app does the rest to itself.

  1. Install the file

    Android will ask whether to allow installs from your browser. Say yes, then open TapSwitch. Setup starts on its own.

  2. Unlock the hidden menu, once

    Android keeps the toggle TapSwitch needs behind a developer options menu that stays hidden until you tap your phone's version number seven times. TapSwitch opens the right screen and names the exact row to tap, in your phone maker's own words.

  3. Turn on Wireless debugging

    The app opens the settings screen for you and tells you what to tap. You turn the switch on, choose "Pair device with pairing code", and type the six digits into the notification TapSwitch puts in your shade.

  4. Pick your two cards

    Your access card app and your tap-to-pay app. TapSwitch turns Wireless debugging back off behind you and starts working. You do not open it again.

What it does not do

The things that would annoy you later, said now.

  • The borrowing card only holds the slot while its own app is on screen. If you set your access card as the borrower, the door needs the screen on with that app open.
  • Built and used daily on Oppo ColorOS. Any Android 12 or newer phone should work, but nothing else has been tested.
  • Setup needs Wi-Fi and about eight taps in a settings menu most people have never opened.
  • On phones that refuse the quick permission, TapSwitch installs itself over the top during setup. Your phone may ask you to confirm that install, and the app closes and reopens once. Nothing is lost.
  • Payment is by hand. You send a message from inside the app, you get a link back, you tap it. Usually within a day.
