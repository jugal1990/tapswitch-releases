What is actually going wrong

Your phone emulates contactless cards in software, and Android will let exactly one app answer a reader at a time. That choice lives in a single system setting called nfc_payment_default_component.

Set your building's access card as the default and tap-to-pay quietly stops working at the till. Set Google Wallet as the default and the door reader ignores you. Stock Android has a "use the payment app while it is open" behaviour for exactly this, and ColorOS does not expose it, so you end up in the NFC settings several times a day.

Nothing is broken and nothing is misconfigured. There is just one slot and two cards that both want it.

What TapSwitch does

You choose which card rests

Whichever one you pick holds the NFC slot all day. Put your access card there and doors behave as they always did. Put Google Wallet there and tap-to-pay is always armed. Neither choice is the "right" one. It depends which one you use without thinking.

The other one borrows it, briefly

When the other card's app comes to the front, the slot is handed straight over. Leave that app and it goes back on its own, without being asked.

Screen timeouts at the till

If your screen sleeps while the borrowing app is open, that card stays ready for about a minute, so waking the phone in front of the reader still works.

There is nothing to open

You set the two cards up once. After that TapSwitch has no screen you need to visit and no setting you need to change. It is doing its job when you forget it exists.

Watch the setting change

The setting in the middle is the real one. nfc_payment_default_component is the single value Android reads to decide which of your cards answers a reader, and those two component names are what it actually holds.

Wallet opens, the value changes and the slot goes blue. Wallet closes, the access card takes it back. Nothing is tapped in TapSwitch itself, because there is nothing in TapSwitch to tap.

The first half rests on the access card. Halfway through it switches over and runs the same thing the other way, with Google Wallet resting and the access card borrowing. Which one you rest on is a setting, not a limitation.

Installing it

Setup needs a computer once, and there is no way around that. ColorOS refuses to let the permission TapSwitch needs be granted on the phone alone, so a small installer grants it over USB. After that the app runs by itself and updates never need a computer again.

  1. Turn on developer optionsSettings, About device, Version, then tap Build number seven times.
  2. Turn on USB debuggingSettings, Additional settings, Developer options.
  3. Plug the phone into your computerAny USB cable that carries data.
  4. Run the installer from the zipInstall-TapSwitch-Mac.command or Install-TapSwitch-Windows.bat.
  5. Tap Allow on the phoneThe app opens with four green checks. Make sure the switch at the top is on.

Questions

Can Google Wallet be the default instead of my access card?

Yes. Either card can be the one that rests in the NFC slot; you pick during setup and can change it later. If Wallet rests, your access card takes the slot only while its own app is on screen. Both directions work exactly the same way. The only question is which card you use more often.

Which phones does this work on?

Oppo phones running ColorOS on Android 12 or newer. It was built and tested on a Find X9 running ColorOS 16. Other Android phones may work and are untested. You are welcome to try it and say what happened.

Why does it need a computer to install?

Writing nfc_payment_default_component requires a permission Android will not let an app request from inside itself. On most phones you can grant it with one command; ColorOS blocks that too, and the only route left is a granting install over USB. The installer in the zip does it and then gets out of the way.

Does it drain the battery?

It checks which app is in front and writes one setting when that changes. There is no network traffic, no location, and no wake locks beyond a brief hold at the till.

What does it cost?

Free for 14 days, and the clock starts the first time it actually runs rather than when you download it. After that there is a one-off fee for a permanent unlock, with no subscription and no account. It is tied to your handset. When the trial ends TapSwitch puts your access card back as the default, so your doors keep working exactly as before. Only the automation stops.

Does it see what apps I use?

It has to know when your payment app is in front, so it reads the foreground app. Nothing leaves the phone. The diagnostic log records any app that is not your configured pay or access app as (other), so even a bug report you send cannot carry your app usage.

Will it work in a cloned app or Second Space?

No, and it refuses to try. Android keeps NFC settings separate per profile, so a copy running there could not change the card your phone actually presents.

How do updates work?

Install over the top. Settings, permissions and your unlock are all kept, and no computer is involved. The app tells you under About when a newer version exists, or you can point Obtainium at the releases repository.
